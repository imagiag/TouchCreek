This is Creek

A sample-free procedural creek for the Synthux Touch synthesizer and Daisy Seed, from a quiet trickle to a lively shallow stream. Nothing is recorded: hundreds of tiny bubbles per second, slow eddies, small splashes and fine surface ticks, all driven by one slowly changing creek state. Switch to Notes and play it: tap a pad for a tuned drop, hold it and the water pours onto the note.

Each bubble is a short decaying tone whose pitch comes from its size, f₀ ≈ 3 / r (Kees van den Doel, Physically-based Models for Liquid Sounds, 2005): small bubbles are high, big ones low.

Quick Install

Download the newest file from releases/ (its README lists what each version changes) and flash it with the Daisy web programmer in Chrome or Edge:

  • Unplugged: hold BOOT while plugging in USB
  • Already powered: hold BOOT, tap RESET, release BOOT

Then Connect → DFU in FS Mode → choose the file → Program. To go back, flash any Synthux firmware the same way.

USB is MIDI. For measuring CPU there's a separate monitor build (see Emulator & Monitor); it isn't part of the releases.

Every version is kept in releases/ with a name saying what changed, so you can always flash an older one. build/CreekTouch.bin is a working build and may be newer than any release.

Controls

At power-up the creek plays whatever the knobs and faders are set to; the second-layer controls start at their defaults (natural width, default tone, attack and release, filter open). The left switch decides what the pads do.

Switches

Left Switch — Mode
  • Up: PRESETS (P03–P06 recall the four presets)
  • Center: NOTES (P03–P09 play the scale; Free flow and Variety apply)
  • Down: DIAGNOSTICS (P03–P07 mute layers, P08 all on)
Right Switch — Latch / Hold

  • Down in Notes: Latch. Notes you touch keep pouring after you let go (up to 4); tap a latched note again to release it; switching Latch off releases everything you're not touching
  • Down in Presets / Diagnostics: Hold the slow creek state. Flow, turbulence and air stop changing so you can compare settings; bubbles keep happening

Creek Synthesis & Expression

Everything you hear is made of short decaying tones; there is no noise in the sound for now.

  • Bubbles: the main voice. Up to 24 at once, mostly small and quiet, now and then a larger, lower and louder one. About a third rise slightly in pitch as they fade, like real bubbles leaving the surface. Their size sets their pitch (f₀ ≈ 3 / r), never a fixed note list, so the creek doesn't turn into a melody unless you ask it to.
  • Three places, one creek: the open flow (centre), the wake behind a rock (slightly left) and a small drop (slightly right). Each has its own energy, turbulence and trapped air, following the knobs with a little inertia (0.1–0.35 s) and drifting on their own. They share causes, not timing, so surges feel connected without everything pulsing at once. Bubbles gather in the wake and the drop.
  • Eddies: two to four overlapping slow swells (0.3–3 s; slower with larger Scale, deeper with Roughness) in the rock wake. They have no sound of their own: each one makes the left side surge with more, slightly bigger and lower bubbles, then settle.
  • Small waterfalls: at the drop, 0.2–4 times a second as Flow rises: a quick fizz of 3–7 tiny bubbles (2.5–10 kHz) within 20 ms, then 2–8 bubbles settling over the next tenth of a second (more with Aeration), and a short burst of extra air in the drop. Rougher water gives more uneven splashes. Overlapping splashes share a limited energy, so they never swamp the creek.
  • Surface detail: sparse, very short, quiet high ticks (2–9 kHz), more near the drop, at turbulence peaks and right after splashes, in loose bursts rather than a steady crackle. In Notes mode the ticks and the falls' fizz are tuned to the held notes (in their own high octaves; Variety spreads them over the scale), and stay on the last played notes after you let go, until you play new ones.
  • Filter: hold P11 and move the faders: right = cutoff, left = resonance. A smooth 12 dB/oct low-pass on both channels (the same one as TouchPulsar), before the output limiter, so strong resonance can't clip. At power-up it's fully open.
  • Throw a stone (P01, any mode; MIDI CC 29): a deep plop that falls in pitch (lower with larger Scale), a burst of larger bubbles spreading out from the centre over a third of a second, and a disturbance that settles over 2–3 s: air in every part of the creek, more bubbling and splashing, ripples of surface ticks. In still water (Free flow at zero) it stirs the water up and lets it go still again. Pad velocity sets the size of the stone.
  • Hold (right switch down outside Notes): freezes the slow creek state while bubbles keep coming, to compare one setting with another.
  • Output: a DC blocker and a linked stereo limiter keep peaks below −1 dBFS; at 0 dB level the presets peak around −5 to −7 dBFS, so the limiter only catches rare overlaps.
  • Soft Takeover: after a preset, after a MIDI CC, or when you press or release P10 / P11, a knob only takes control once you move it, so values never jump. The emulator shows it: the gold arc is the value in use, the white pointer the knob.

Playing Notes

  • Drops: tapping a note pad (or a MIDI key) drops one bubble tuned to the note, with 1–3 quieter bubbles at the same note or an octave up, like a drop falling into a pool. Velocity sets how loud.
  • Pouring: holding a note makes the water pour onto it: bubbles in the rhythm of Flow, Roughness and Aeration, rising in about a quarter second and settling about half a second after you let go. Splash bubbles from the drop join in. Hold up to 4 notes for a chord.
  • Chords: hold up to 4 notes at once (pads and MIDI keys together) and the water pours onto all of them: each bubble lands on whichever held note is nearest its own pitch, so a chord shimmers as one cloud. Variety spreads within the scale around them. Holding a 5th note releases the oldest one (it settles with Release). With Latch, tapped notes add up to a 4-note chord you can leave pouring.
  • Free flow (S30): fully left, the creek is still and only your notes sound; turn it up and the creek plays by itself underneath, its own bubbles gathering onto the held notes too.
  • Variety (S35): left of noon pulls everything onto the held note itself (fully left: one note, a pure rhythmic pattern from the flow). Right of noon spreads the bubbles over more and more notes of the active scale, up to an octave around the held note, so a held note becomes a shimmering cloud in the scale.
  • Attack (P11 + S33) and Release (P11 + S34), both on P11 (ranges and CCs as TouchPulsar): Attack sets how quickly a held note's pour swells in (4 ms = at once, 3 s = a slow tide), Release how long it keeps pouring and settling after you let go (20 ms–4 s), still on the notes you played.
  • Decay (S34): short, dry plinks to long ringing tones.
  • Tone (P11 + S31): darker or brighter over a wide range: first the mix of big and small bubbles, then near the ends all bubbles move up to an octave (noon = default).
  • Latch (right switch down): notes keep pouring hands-free; tap one again to stop it.
  • Surge (MIDI mod wheel or aftertouch): the water rushes: Flow, Roughness and a little Aeration rise on top of the knob settings, a crescendo you can play.
  • Range: tuned notes fold by octaves into about 41 Hz – 10 kHz; larger Scale (and a darker Tone) brings the low end in.

LED Indicator

The onboard LED provides visual feedback:

  • Bubbles: flickers with notes and the louder bubbles (in Diagnostics it stays lit and dips on them).
  • Mode: 1 blink = Presets, 2 = Notes, 3 = Diagnostics when the left switch moves.
  • Preset: 1–4 blinks for the preset number.
  • Scale Confirmation: blinks N times for scale 1 through 7 (Chromatic = 1 blink, …, Harmonic Minor = 7 blinks).
  • Octave Shift: quick blinks: 1 at octave 0, 2 at ±1, 3 at +2.
  • Root Key: 1 long blink.
  • Stone: a longer flash when a stone is thrown.
  • Diagnostics: 2 blinks = layer on, 1 blink = layer muted, 3 quick blinks = all on (monitor build: stress test = 4 quick blinks, then fast blinking while it runs).

MIDI

Creek appears as a USB MIDI device (listed as Creek) when connected via the Daisy Seed's USB port to a computer, DAW, iPad or USB-MIDI host. A keyboard plugged straight into the Touch won't work: both are USB devices, so route it through the computer or a host box. It listens on all MIDI channels.

  • Notes: play like the note pads, in every mode: a tuned drop on press, the water pours onto the note while held. Fully chromatic, separate from the pad scale. Up to 4 held notes (pads and MIDI together). In Notes mode, Free flow and Latch apply.
  • Velocity: drop level. Channel aftertouch: Surge.
  • Pitch Bend: ±2 semitones (drops and pouring).
  • Sustain (CC 64): holds released notes.
  • Mod Wheel (CC 1): Surge: the water rushes, raising Flow, Roughness and a little Aeration on top of the knobs; back at zero it changes nothing.
  • Program Change 1–4: the four presets.

Emulator & Monitor

  • Browser emulator: python3 web/serve.py 8766, then open http://localhost:8766. The same panel and the same engine (a JavaScript twin, tested against the firmware's), with pickup, layers and LED behaving as on the hardware. Every knob shows its name, S-number, second layer and value. Enable MIDI plays it from a keyboard on your computer; Render 30 s WAV saves what you hear.
  • Monitor (also for debugging): the emulator's Monitor button opens it for the emulator; for the Touch, build it with make monitor in daisy/ (USB sends the report instead of MIDI, and Diagnostics P09 becomes a stress test), flash build-monitor/CreekTouch-monitor.bin, open http://localhost:8766/monitor.html in Chrome or Edge, plug in the Touch and click Connect. It shows which layers are sounding and how much (bubble voices and rate, played drops / stones, eddies, splash plops, surface ticks, mutes, held notes), the last pressed pad / knob / switch with its meaning, CPU load (average and worst 1 ms block), stack use, dropped events and limiter activity. For the worst case use Diagnostics + P09 (stress test); measured on the Touch it stays under 25 % (limit 70 %).

Project Setup

Needs the Daisy toolchain. libDaisy is a git submodule in daisy/lib/libDaisy (Synthux's fork, pinned); DaisySP isn't needed. Clone with submodules, then build from daisy/:

$ git clone --recurse-submodules <repository URL> creek
$ cd creek/daisy
$ make libs -j8
$ make clean; make -j8
$ make program-dfu
  • make monitor builds build-monitor/CreekTouch-monitor.bin (only when needed): USB sends the CPU report instead of MIDI, and Diagnostics P09 runs a stress test.
  • make -C test checks the engine on your computer and compares its sound with the browser engine in ../web; make -C test soak runs a 30-minute stability test; make -C test renders writes reference WAVs of every scenario.
  • All tuning constants live in web/engine/creek-config.js; node tools/gen-config.js writes the C++ copy.
